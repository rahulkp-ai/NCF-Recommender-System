# Chapter 4 — Research Methodology

## Chapter Overview

This chapter specifies the methodological design of the study: the overall research strategy, the workflow connecting its research and production components, the experimental hypotheses derived from the research questions of Section 1.8, the variables held constant or manipulated across experimental conditions, the protocol under which experiments were executed, the reproducibility measures adopted, and the hardware and software environment in which the reported results were obtained. Consistent with the evidentiary discipline established in Chapters 1–3, this chapter distinguishes explicitly between what was methodologically designed (proposed protocol) and what is confirmed by the project's actual configuration files, logs, and notebooks (implemented protocol), flagging any point at which the two could not be fully reconciled from the available artefacts.

## 4.1 Research Design

The study combines two distinct research strategies, applied to different components of the overall investigation, rather than a single uniform experimental design across all chapters.

The first strategy, applied to Chapters 6–8, is **constructive research with controlled empirical comparison**. A mathematical formulation is specified (Chapter 2), realised in two independent implementations — a from-scratch NumPy implementation and a PyTorch implementation — under a shared data pipeline and, to the extent achievable, matched hyperparameters, and the two implementations are then compared on a fixed set of quantitative outcome measures (final training loss, validation Hit Rate, wall-clock training time). This is a single-configuration, two-condition comparison rather than a designed multi-factor experiment: no hyperparameter sweep or ablation study was conducted, a scope limitation stated explicitly in `research/README.md` and carried forward from Chapter 1's scope statement (Section 1.11).

The second strategy, applied to Chapter 9, is **behavioural case demonstration**. Rather than a quantitative comparison against baseline models, the hybrid recommendation engine's decision logic is exercised against representative user states (a cold-start user, a partially warm user, a fully warm user) and its output — selected recommendation source and mixing coefficient — is recorded and interpreted qualitatively. This strategy is appropriate to the available evidence (Section 3.6) but is more limited in evidentiary strength than a quantitative baseline comparison, a distinction maintained explicitly rather than conflated.

The third strategy, applied to Chapters 10–12, is **systems/design-science evaluation**: the production architecture is assessed against its own documented design goals (research-production separation, observability, testability) using the implemented code, configuration, and test suite as evidence, rather than through a controlled experiment or live deployment measurement.

## 4.2 Research Workflow

The overall research workflow proceeds through six methodological stages, each corresponding to a cluster of thesis chapters:

1. **Theoretical formulation** (Chapter 2): specification of the NCF prediction function, loss function, and backpropagation equations, independent of any specific implementation.
2. **Data preparation** (Chapter 5): acquisition, cleaning, encoding, and splitting of the MovieLens-1M dataset into training, validation, and test partitions, with negative sampling applied to construct the implicit-feedback training signal.
3. **From-scratch implementation and verification** (Chapter 6): realisation of the MLP-based NCF variant in NumPy, with numerical gradient verification of the resulting backward pass.
4. **Framework implementation and parity assessment** (Chapter 7): realisation of an architecturally matched model in PyTorch, and comparison against the from-scratch implementation.
5. **Experimental evaluation** (Chapter 8): quantitative benchmarking of both implementations on ranking-quality and computational-performance metrics.
6. **Extension to hybrid recommendation and production integration** (Chapters 9–12): behavioural demonstration of cold-start handling, and integration of the resulting components into a documented, containerised, tested system.

This workflow is deliberately sequential — each stage's output is a direct input to the next — and the thesis narrative (Chapter 1, Section 1.12) is structured to mirror it. Where the actual project artefacts depart from strict sequential execution (for example, the production `recommenders/` directory is a hardened port of the research-stage hybrid engine per ADR 0002, rather than a wholly separate development effort), this is noted at the relevant point in Chapters 9 and 10 rather than obscured by the workflow diagram.

## 4.3 Experimental Hypotheses

Three experimental hypotheses are stated here, directly derived from the research questions of Section 1.8, to be evaluated against the results reported in Chapter 8 and interpreted in Chapter 13. Consistent with the research-integrity commitments of this thesis, these hypotheses are stated as **proposed, testable expectations formulated prior to result interpretation**, and their confirmation, partial confirmation, or disconfirmation is determined strictly by the logged numerical evidence examined in Chapter 8 — no hypothesis is treated as confirmed by default, and none is evaluated using formal inferential statistics (e.g., significance testing), since the underlying experiments consist of single training runs per condition rather than repeated trials, a limitation stated explicitly here and revisited in Chapter 14.

**H1 (Scratch–PyTorch behavioural consistency).** A from-scratch NumPy implementation of the MLP-based NCF variant, trained under a shared data pipeline and matched core hyperparameters (embedding dimensionality, hidden-layer sizes, learning rate) to an architecturally equivalent PyTorch implementation, will converge to a final training loss and validation Hit Rate@10 within a small, practically comparable margin of the PyTorch implementation's corresponding values, over an equal number of training epochs.

**H2 (Computational overhead of the from-scratch implementation).** The from-scratch NumPy implementation will require substantially greater wall-clock training time than the PyTorch implementation, owing to the absence of vectorised, hardware-accelerated tensor operations and the absence of GPU/accelerator execution in the from-scratch code path.

**H3 (Hybrid strategy cold-start coverage).** The hybrid recommendation engine's mixing coefficient will exhibit the designed monotonic dependence on user interaction count, such that users with no or minimal interaction history are served predominantly by the cold-start (popularity/content) score, users with substantial interaction history are served predominantly by the NCF score, and users with intermediate interaction counts receive a blended score, as observed across representative user states rather than as a population-level statistical claim.

No hypothesis concerning quantitative hybrid-versus-single-signal-baseline accuracy is stated, because, as established in Section 3.6 and the project audit, no baseline Hit Rate/NDCG evaluation against the hybrid engine's constituent signals in isolation exists among the project's artefacts; RQ3 is accordingly addressed in this thesis at the level of qualitative/behavioural evidence (H3) rather than quantitative accuracy improvement.

The unconfirmed optimiser identity and the confirmed batch-size and compute-device differences are treated in this thesis as genuine methodological limitations rather than being elided: the batch-size difference (512 vs 1024) affects the number of gradient update steps per epoch, and the compute-device difference (CPU-only NumPy execution vs GPU-accelerated MPS execution) means that the wall-clock training-time comparison examined under H2 reflects both an implementation difference (hand-derived NumPy operations vs a mature, hardware-accelerated tensor library) and a hardware-acceleration difference, and the two factors cannot be disentangled from the available artefacts. This point is stated explicitly here so that the interpretation offered in Chapter 8 and Chapter 13 does not overstate what the training-time comparison alone can support: the reported speedup is evidence of the _combined_ effect of implementation and hardware, not of the from-scratch implementation's algorithmic efficiency in isolation.

## 4.5 Experimental Protocol

The experimental protocol executed for the scratch-versus-PyTorch comparison (Chapters 6–8) proceeds as follows, as reconstructed from the project's data pipeline, training scripts, and logged configuration:

1. The MovieLens-1M dataset is loaded and preprocessed by `movielens_loader.py`, producing user/item integer encodings, a constructed implicit-feedback interaction set, and negative samples at a 1:4 positive-to-negative ratio (Chapter 5).
2. The resulting training, validation, and test partitions are persisted (`research/datasets/processed/`) and consumed identically by both the scratch and PyTorch training scripts.
3. The from-scratch model is trained for 20 epochs under the configuration recorded in `scratch_ncf_log.json`, with per-epoch loss and periodic validation Hit Rate (computed on a 200-user subsample) logged to disk, and final weights persisted to `scratch_ncf_weights.npz`.
4. The PyTorch model is trained for 20 epochs under the configuration recorded in `pytorch_ncf_log.json`, on the `mps` device, with analogous per-epoch logging and final weights persisted to `pytorch_ncf_weights.pt`.
5. `compare.py` consumes both log files and computes derived comparison statistics (per-epoch and total speedup, final-loss delta, best-Hit@10 delta), persisted to `comparison.json`.
6. The PyTorch model is additionally evaluated once against the full test set (6,040 users) via `evaluate.py`, producing `pytorch_test_results.json` (Hit@10, NDCG@10); no equivalent full-test-set evaluation artefact exists for the scratch model among the audited files.
7. Separately, `research/notebooks/03_model_parity_verification.ipynb` performs a single forward-pass comparison between the two models at independent, non-matched random initialisations (scratch seed 42; PyTorch default initialisation), intended as a sanity check on structural equivalence rather than as a controlled, same-weights parity test.

This protocol is reported here exactly as reconstructed from the audited artefacts, without inferring steps that are not directly evidenced. In particular, no evidence of a repeated-run (multiple-seed) protocol was found; each condition (scratch, PyTorch) corresponds to a single logged training run, a constraint that limits the statistical strength of any comparison drawn from Table 4.1's dependent variables and is stated explicitly in Section 4.3 and revisited in Chapter 14.

## 4.6 Reproducibility Strategy

The project's reproducibility strategy, as evidenced by its repository structure and documentation, rests on four mechanisms:

**Deterministic seeding at specific, documented points.** A fixed random seed (42) is used for the scratch model's initialisation in the parity-verification notebook (`03_model_parity_verification.ipynb`). Seeding of the two full 20-epoch training runs that produced `scratch_ncf_log.json` and `pytorch_ncf_log.json` is not independently confirmed from the audited configuration files; this is noted as an evidence gap below rather than assumed.

**Persisted intermediate artefacts.** Processed dataset splits (`research/datasets/processed/`), trained model weights (`scratch_ncf_weights.npz`, `pytorch_ncf_weights.pt`), and training logs (`scratch_ncf_log.json`, `pytorch_ncf_log.json`) are persisted to disk rather than regenerated implicitly at analysis time, allowing the comparison and evaluation scripts (`compare.py`, `evaluate.py`) to be re-run against a fixed set of prior outputs.

**Version-controlled, containerised production environment.** The production system's reproducibility relies on Docker and Docker Compose definitions (`deployment/docker/`, `docker-compose.yml`) that pin service composition, and on GitHub Actions CI workflows (`ci.yaml`) that execute linting, type-checking, and the automated test suite on a defined environment, providing a mechanism for verifying that the codebase remains in a runnable, tested state independent of any individual developer's local environment.

**Explicit documentation of what is and is not reproducible.** The project's own documentation — the `research/README.md` statement that no hyperparameter search or ablation was performed, and the Kubernetes/Terraform README files stating that those artefacts are reference scaffolding rather than applied infrastructure — is treated in this thesis as part of its reproducibility evidence, since it delineates precisely which claims a third party could expect to reproduce from the repository as-is (the training runs, the test suite, the containerised local deployment) versus which claims would require additional infrastructure not present in the repository (a live Kubernetes cluster, a completed hyperparameter search).

## Chapter Summary

This chapter has specified the study's methodological design: a constructive, single-configuration empirical comparison for the from-scratch/PyTorch investigation (H1, H2), a behavioural case-demonstration methodology for the hybrid/cold-start investigation (H3), and a systems/design-science evaluation methodology for the production architecture. It has stated three explicit, falsifiable hypotheses tied directly to the research questions of Chapter 1, enumerated the experimental variables involved in the central scratch-versus-PyTorch comparison — including two confirmed confounds (batch size, compute device) and one unconfirmed variable (optimiser identity for the scratch run) that qualify how strongly H1 and H2 can be interpreted — described the reconstructed experimental protocol, and detailed the project's reproducibility mechanisms and software environment. The explicit identification of unconfirmed and uncontrolled variables in this chapter is a deliberate methodological commitment: it ensures that the results reported in Chapter 8 and interpreted in Chapter 13 are read against an accurate account of what was, and was not, held constant between the two implementations under comparison.
